Wonder

2026-07-23 · 4 min read reflections
La meraviglia è quella incredibile combinazione di qualcosa che vediamo che contamina soltanto i nostri occhi, che adatta soltanto alla nostra anima, al nostro cuore. Wonder is that incredible combination of something we see that contaminates only our eyes, that adapts only to our soul, to our heart.

The verb is usually negative. Contaminate means something you didn't choose, something that spreads without permission, that alters you from the inside. Applied to wonder it becomes exactly right. You don't decide to feel it. It finds the precise crack in your attention and invades, adapting to the specific architecture of who you are at that moment. Not contagion in the mass-transmission sense. Something more personal: it contaminates only your eyes. Nobody else sees quite the same thing, because nobody else brings quite the same history to what you're looking at.

This is why wonder is so hard to share.

You can point at the thing that caused it. You can describe what you saw. But the contamination happened in the gap between the thing and you, in a space made entirely of your own experience, and that space isn't transferable. Alexandra Horowitz walked the same city block with eleven different experts and found eleven different worlds: a geologist, a doctor, an artist, a dog. Same street, same hour. What each person saw was entirely determined by what they'd trained themselves to notice. The other person looks at the same object and sees something correct, something fine. The wonder was in the fit, and the fit was yours.

Christopher McCandless, dying alone in Alaska, wrote in the margins of his journal: "Happiness only real when shared." The opposite pull. Wonder contaminates only your eyes; happiness demands company to be real at all. Both are true about intimate experience, and the tension between them is what makes the drive to write, to build, to point at the thing and say: look, this way. To feel that necessary. You can't transfer the contamination. But you can gesture at where it happened.

The virtual experience

Working with AI produces this more often than I expected, and in a specific way. The prompt that actually works is rarely the clever one. It's the one that fits the exact shape of the problem as you hold it, with your specific vocabulary, your specific framing, the half-formed intuition you haven't fully articulated yet. Someone copies the same prompt and gets a competent output. You get the one that makes you stop, because what came back was the thought you were reaching for, made legible. That gap is where the contamination happens. It's intimate almost by definition, because the conditions that created it were yours alone.

Building has the same property. When I noticed that CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR was the seam between textaccounts, textserve, and textsessions, that wasn't planned. It leaked. Each tool was built for its own problem, and the right constraint spread on its own, without needing to be carried. The moment it clicked, the three-command sequence snapping into place, felt like something being revealed rather than something being made. A private revelation. The architecture had been waiting in the logic of the tools; I just had to build long enough to see it. That's the intimate kind of wonder: the thing that only makes sense given exactly the path you walked to find it.

But the model was contaminated first.

Training data is saturated with human wonder: centuries of science, poetry, obsession, discovery, the record of what happened to people when something astonished them. The model absorbed not just the knowledge but the shape of what wonder produces. When it generates something that stops you, it's partly because it learned from a vast archive of humans being stopped by things, and internalized what that moment looks like from the inside. The wonder in the output is a reflection. Your species wrote it down, and the model learned to recognize and reproduce the form.

Which makes the direction interesting. You think you're the one experiencing wonder at the model's output. But the model was contaminated by wonder before you arrived. Every session adds to that, in a diffuse way. Every moment you push back, reframe, go deeper, you're leaving a trace of how a specific person thinks when a specific thing surprises them. The contamination runs both ways: you bring your architecture to the model, the model returns something shaped by all the architectures that came before, and neither side is passive in what happens next.

There's a collective version of this too. An eclipse infects many people simultaneously because it touches something universal, a shared threshold where the familiar becomes strange. The big AI demos work the same way: the moment spreads because it triggers a common cord, the latent sense that something fundamental just shifted. That's real. It matters. But it's a different thing from the intimate kind, and it doesn't replace it.

The collective wonder points at scale. The intimate kind points at fit. And fit is quiet: a session that resumes exactly where your thinking was, a tool that handles exactly the layer you needed handled, a response that arrives in your vocabulary and not someone else's. The contamination that actually alters you isn't the impressive demo. It's the smaller thing, the one that adapted to only your eyes, that you couldn't fully explain to anyone else, that changed how you saw the problem and therefore changed how you built the next thing.

That's the kind worth building for.
